Mobility flows reveal an overlapping mesoscale in 50 US cities

Cities are usually divided into disjoint regions, yet many places serve several of them. From the trips between census block groups, a graph neural network infers how strongly each place belongs to each of ten communities. These weighted, overlapping memberships reproduce a city's flows and generate the flows of places they were not fitted to.

Two representations of one system

Memberships generate flows

The model approximates the logarithm of the trips between two block groups by the inner product of their memberships. Two places that belong strongly to the same communities have many trips between them; a place shared by two communities has many trips with both. Choose any block group on the map to see the flows its memberships imply.

Flow reconstruction on observed pairs · Fig. 2

Weights are needed to reproduce the flows

Every representation is converted into a flow model whose weights are fitted under the same error, L1, the mean absolute difference between predicted and observed log flows. The weighted memberships F have a lower L1 than their own binary and partitioned versions, nine existing community-detection methods and two node embeddings, in every one of the 50 cities.

Flow generation for held-out origins · Fig. 3

Memberships as place features

Origins were split at random into training, validation and test sets five times. For each split, F was re-estimated from the flows leaving the training origins, and Deep Gravity generated the outflows of the test origins. With F as place features, the common part of commuters (CPC) and the Spearman correlation were higher than with population, points of interest or both, and than the radiation and four gravity models, in every city.

What shared places offer · Fig. 5

Communities overlap where higher-order functions concentrate

Before the analysis, 30 point-of-interest categories were assigned by name to a higher-order group (such as comparison retail, hotels and specialty hospitals) and an everyday group (such as schools, groceries and child day care), following the central-place order of goods. Places shared by several communities hold higher-order categories more often, and their inbound trips start in a more income-mixed set of neighbourhoods.

Odds that a point of interest of each category lies in the overlap zone rather than the rest of its city, relative to all other points of interest (log2 odds ratio): median over cities (dot) and interquartile range (line). Rings show the chosen city.

Central-place contrast G

G, the mean log2 odds ratio of the higher-order categories minus that of the everyday ones; positive in all 50 cities.

Income mix of trip origins

Difference in Hinc, the income-quintile entropy of the block groups where a block group's inbound trips start, between the overlap zone and the rest; higher in the overlap zone in 49 of 50 cities.
